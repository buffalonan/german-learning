# AGENTS.md

Context for AI agents and reviewers working on this repo. Read this first instead of re-deriving it from the code. Keep it up to date when you change how things work.

## What this is

Web games for two adult learners to practise everyday German. Both were once B1 level and are now rusty. The focus is vocabulary, useful daily phrases and listening. Translations are shown in English and Spanish.

They play together on one laptop, racing to answer first, or on two phones linked by a room code (**Zwei Handys**, see below). The app has four areas, switched by the tabs at the top: **Wortkarten** (Sag's auf Deutsch: picture cards where you say the German word, then flip to check, in 23 topics; `time` shows a Spanish cue on the front like the phrases, since days and clock times can't be told apart by picture alone), **Grammatik** (short theory pages for Akkusativ & Dativ, prepositions, the Perfekt (haben or sein, participles), separable verbs, and the B1 topics weil/dass/wenn/obwohl, Präteritum, polite Konjunktiv II and Komparativ, each with a gap-fill quiz) and **Hören** (a listening game modelled on the Goethe A2/B1 Hören part: the game reads an announcement, voicemail, conversation or radio item aloud and the players pick the right answer; the B1 "Interviews" kind asks richtig/falsch, and the B1 "Führungen" kind is a guide or speaker talking for four or five sentences, like Hören Teil 2) and **Prüfung** (the other Goethe A2/B1 exam parts: two speaking tasks, reading and writing).

Roadmap: (1) picture card game, done; (2) bigger word bank and more everyday sentences; (3) session setup with listening and writing modes; (4) installable phone version, then an Android app.

## How the game code works (index.html)

- **Word bank:** the `TOPICS` array. Each topic is `{ id, name (German), en, icon, items, cue? }`. Each card is `[front, german, article, english, spanish, feminine?]`.
  - `front` is an emoji, a `#rrggbb` colour swatch, or `n:12` for a numeral.
  - A card with a painted illustration in `art/<topic id>/<artName(german)>.webp` (listed in `art.js`) shows it instead, on a rounded tile with no pastel disc. Every card has one (numbers have brush-painted numerals; the 149 cards added in November 2026 were painted from the prompts in `illustrations/new-topics-batch.md`, the 86 Goethe cards from `illustrations/goethe-batch.md`), so the emoji picture, colour swatch and plain numeral are only fallbacks, and their tests use made-up cards. `artName` lowercases, turns ä/ö/ü/ß into ae/oe/ue/ss and other characters into `-` ("sich setzen" → `sich-setzen`), and must match `art_name` in `scripts/add-art.py`. Illustrations are keyed by topic and word, not emoji, because some emoji are shared (🪑 is both "sich setzen" and "Stuhl").
  - The topic, lesson and listening tiles show `art/tiles/<id>.webp` when `art.js` lists `tiles/<id>` (`tileIcon`), otherwise the `icon` emoji, so tile ids must be unique across `TOPICS`, `GRAMMAR` and `LISTENING`. The end-of-round "Worth another look" list shows each missed card's painting too.
  - `article` is `der`, `die`, `das`, `pl` (shown as "die" with a Pl. mark) or `""` for none (verbs, colours, numbers, phrases).
  - `feminine` is the feminine job form, e.g. `"die Lehrerin"`.
  - `cue: true` (phrases) always shows a text cue on the front.
  - The 86 cards added for the Goethe exam topics (`office`, `media`, `school`, `environment`, and the months and heute/gestern/vorgestern/übermorgen in `time`) were painted from `illustrations/goethe-batch.md` in November 2026. The months are one painted row of twelve blocks (with the Januar snowman) where each month lights up its own block, and gestern/übermorgen were derived from the arrow pictures with Pillow.
  - Articles are colour-coded everywhere: der blue, die red, das green, plural brown. The card back's border takes the article's colour too.
- **Grammar:** the `GRAMMAR` array, one lesson per entry `{ id, name, en, icon, items }`. Each item is `[sentence with ___, answer, [1–3 wrong options], english, spanish, why]`; `why` is the English explanation shown after answering. Every lesson has at least 100 items and the Präpositionen lesson at least 20 each of wohin and wo (tests check both); sentences must be unique across all lessons. The theory text is plain markup in `#theory`, one `<article data-lesson="…">` per lesson (a test checks every lesson has one); a lesson's `icon` must already be in `pictures.js`, since `build-pictures.js` only collects emoji from `TOPICS`; `.ex` buttons there read their sentence aloud. Quiz state is the global `quiz`; `dealQuestion`, `choose`, `nextQuestion` and `finishQuiz` render it. Keys: 1–4 answer, Enter/Space next. "Wettkampf" (`turns`) alternates the players with a point each; "Üben" (`together`) counts right answers. Mode and round size sit above the lessons, with "Weiter: <lesson>" for the lesson played last (`glast`). The translation under the gap waits for "Übersetzung zeigen" or the answer.
- **Listening:** the `LISTENING` array (113 texts in six kinds, November 2026), one kind of text per entry `{ id, name, en, icon, dialogue?, items }`. Each item is `[lines, question, answer, [1–3 wrong options], english, spanish]`; `english`/`spanish` translate the whole text and show after answering with the German transcript. Numbers in `lines` are written as words (a test checks there are no digits) so every voice reads them the same. With `dialogue: true` the lines alternate between two speakers: `sayLines` reads them one after another, the second speaker with the other recorded voice (Kerstin ↔ Thorsten) or the device voice at pitch 0.7. Without `dialogue` (e.g. `tours`) several lines are read by the same voice, one after another, which keeps each utterance short. Any other `say()` or `hush()` bumps `talkRun`, which stops a running conversation. A kind added later is switched on for players whose saved `lkinds` predate it: `lknown` in the settings lists the kinds they had seen. State is the global `listen`; `dealText`, `renderListen`, `lchoose`, `reveal`, `nextText`, `finishListening` render it. Practice (default): a wrong option is marked and you try again; "Gewusst" counts right-first-time. Race: options wait for a buzz (A/L or the name buttons), a right answer is a point, a wrong one passes the turn once to the other player, N shows the answer. Keys: R or Space replays, 1–3 answer, Enter next.
- **Prüfung:** `EXAM_TASKS` lists four tasks on the `exam` screen. *Termin finden* (A2 Sprechen): `diaries(code)` builds two diaries for one day (`DIARY_HOURS`, errands from `ERRANDS`, situation from `MEETINGS`) with exactly one hour both are free, seeded by a four-digit task number so two phones can open the same task and each player picks their own name (`#whoSeg`); "Lösung zeigen" names the free hour. *Gemeinsam planen* (B1 Sprechen): `planFor(code)` picks one of `PLANS` with four points to talk through. Both show `SPEAK_PHRASES` as `.ex` buttons that read aloud, and share the `talk` screen. *Lesen*: 8 random items from `READING` (`[text, question, answer, [1–2 wrong], english, spanish]`, the `news` kind is richtig/falsch), practice rules like listening, state in `reading`. *Schreiben*: `WRITING` tasks `{ level, words, de, en, es, points, model, modelEn }`; a live word count, and the model answer after "Musterlösung". Nothing in Prüfung is scored or saved.
- **Screens:** `show(id)` switches between the screens in `SCREENS`; the four sections (`AREAS`: `setup`, `grammar`, `listen`, `exam`) show the tabs and are the top level. Every other screen pushes one history entry, so the phone's or browser's Back runs that screen's own back button (`BACK` maps screen → button); going back to a section from a button pops the entry again. In the Android app `@capacitor/app`'s `backButton` does the same, and closes the app on a section. The app opens on the section used last (`area`).
- **Quick start:** Wortkarten and Hören open on a "Deine Runde" card (summary, Los geht's, Ändern, Runde fortsetzen, Meine Fehler, Zwei Handys). The full settings (`#setupSheet`, `#lsetupSheet`) are open until the first round (`played`, `lplayed`), then fold under Ändern. Mode is "Üben" or "Wettkampf" in every section, with Üben first.
- **Spieler & Stimme** (`prefs` screen, from the `[data-prefs]` link in each section): player names, read aloud, voice and Test. Back returns to the section it came from.
- **Saved rounds** (`sagsDeutsch.rounds`, per `cards`/`quiz`/`listen`): the deck, position, scores and mode, saved on every deal and answer and dropped when the round ends, so "Runde fortsetzen" resumes after leaving, closing the app or a reload. Schreiben keeps a draft per task in `sagsDeutsch.drafts`.
- **Meine Fehler** (`sagsDeutsch.mistakes`): a pile per section of wrong cards (`cardKey`), sentences (`quizKey`) and texts (`textKey`), each leaving after two right answers in later rounds. Lesen is not tracked.
- **Answer screens:** Weiter comes right under the answer and the feedback scrolls into view; the Hören transcript and the Hören/Lesen translations are folded in `<details>` (the transcript opens after a miss). `options()` keeps Richtig before Falsch. On touch screens (`hover: none` and `pointer: coarse`) the key badges `.k` and `.kbd-only` text are hidden and `.touch-only` shows.
- **Zwei Handys** (`duo`, `duoplay` screens): two phones meet through PeerJS's free cloud server (0.peerjs.com) with the peer id `sagsdeutsch-raum-<4 digits>`, then talk over WebRTC. `peerjs.min.js` is loaded only when needed. The phone that made the room is the referee: it deals (`duoStart`), applies both phones' taps (`applyAct`, its own racing taps delayed by half the measured round trip so neither phone has a head start), and sends `{ t: "state", s }` after each change; the other phone only sends `{ t: "act" }`. Cards: first "Ich weiß es!" wins, either phone flips, the other player judges Richtig/Falsch. Hören and Grammatik: the first right option scores, a wrong one locks that player out for the question. Only the referee reads aloud. The real connection can't be tested in CI (no internet), only with the fake.
- **Visitor counter:** a small grey "1.234 Besucher" line under the four sections (`#visits`, `showVisits`), only on the Pages copy: `scripts/build-app-web.js --web` adds `<meta name="visitor-counter" content="buffalonan-german-learning/besucher">`, so the app, the local copy and plain `index.html` never call it. It uses Abacus (`abacus.jasoncameron.dev`, free, no account, no cookies; a counter expires after six months without visits). Each browser counts once (`sagsDeutsch.counted` in `localStorage`): the first visit calls `/hit`, later ones `/get`. Offline, blocked or a bad answer leaves the line hidden. Tests fake the service with `page.route` (`tests/visits.spec.js`).
- **Settings** are kept in `localStorage` under `sagsDeutsch.settings`, always inside try/catch: topics, mode (`practice` by default, or `race`), player names, cards per round (0 means all), front hint (`none`, `en`, `es`), read aloud, voice, `theme` (`""` follows the system, or `light`/`dark` from the round button next to the section tabs; a small script in `<head>` applies it before the page paints), `area`, `played`, `lplayed`, `glast`, `me` (your name on this phone for Zwei Handys), for grammar `gmode` (`turns` or `together`) and `gcount` (0 means all), and for listening `lkinds`, `lknown`, `lmode` (`practice` by default, or `race`) and `lcount` (0 means all).
- **State** is the global `game` (`deck`, `i`, `flipped`, `buzzed`, `scores`, `missed`, `known`, `againCount`). Rendering goes through `dealCard`, `renderScores`, `renderControls` and `finish`.
- **Keys:** Space or Enter flips. Race: A and L buzz in before the flip and award a point after it, N means nobody, Enter awards the player who buzzed. Practice: 1 is Again (the card goes back into the deck three cards later, Ferran's choice in November 2026), 2 is Got it. Once one player buzzes, the other's button is disabled.
- **Recorded voices:** CI records every text the game reads aloud with Piper (voices Kerstin and Thorsten) into `audio/<voice>/<audioName(text)>.mp3`. `spokenTexts()` in the page lists the texts; `scripts/list-spoken.js` asks the page for them and `scripts/build-audio.py` records them; the composite action `.github/actions/recorded-voices` runs both (cached between runs) for the APK and Pages builds. `scripts/build-app-web.js` copies `audio/` and adds `<meta name="recorded-voices" content="kerstin thorsten">`, which is how the page knows they exist; plain `index.html` has no tag and no recordings. They're recorded slowly (`LENGTH_SCALE` 1.4) with low noise and even loudness, because Ferran found the first take too fast and breathy. When recordings exist the menu lists one device voice first ("Google Deutsch" in desktop Chrome, otherwise the best installed German voice, e.g. "German Germany" on Android), then the recordings. That device voice is the default (Ferran asked for it); a recording plays only once picked, or when the device has no German voice. Other device voices are hidden but still read texts without a recording, and a saved hidden voice falls back to the offered one. When a recording is picked, `say()` plays it, or falls back to the device voice below if it's missing. `audio/` is not committed. Tests fake the recordings with `page.route` (`tests/recorded.spec.js`). Changing `audioName` invalidates every recording.
- **Device voice (fallback, or when the player picks one):** the browser's `speechSynthesis`. Among device voices, the default is "Google Deutsch" when it exists (Chrome only), because Chrome on macOS reads text sent to the Mac's own voices (Anna…) with the English system voice. Elsewhere (Safari, iPhone) voices installed on the device are preferred. A voice the player picks is remembered. The voice menu and Test button live on Spieler & Stimme, and a hint appears if nothing starts within 1.8 s. Without recordings the menu lists every German device voice, and with no German voice it offers "Browser default"/"Phone default" (value `device`), and picking it is remembered too.
  - In the Android app (`window.Capacitor.isNativePlatform()`), the WebView has no `speechSynthesis`, so `tts` (the `TextToSpeech` plugin from `@capacitor-community/text-to-speech`) is used instead: `loadNativeVoices` retries while the phone's engine starts, the plugin picks a voice by its index in the full voice list, and `sayNative` reports only the latest call's error. Tests mock it with `mockNativeSpeech`.

## Rules

- **Write markup only through the `html` tagged template.** It escapes every value unless that value was itself built with `html`. Never assign a plain string to `innerHTML` or `outerHTML`. ESLint's `no-unsanitized` rule fails CI if you do.
- Player names and voice names are the only user input. They must stay escaped, and a test covers this.
- Elements are shown and hidden with `el.hidden`. The page's `[hidden] { display: none !important; }` rule is what makes that work, so keep it.
- German must be correct: check the article, the plural and the spelling. Every card needs English and Spanish too.
- A card with a new emoji needs a picture: run `node scripts/build-pictures.js`. The word bank test fails otherwise.
- New illustrations go in with `scripts/add-art.py`, never by hand-editing `art.js`. Prompts and the approved originals are in the project's shared files (`illustrations/`). Each picture must belong to a card, and a test checks this.
- `scripts/build-pictures.js` evaluates the `TOPICS` literal from this repo's own `index.html` with `Function`. That's a deliberate exception for a local build script. The no-`eval` rule is about the game code.
- New or changed behaviour needs a Playwright test. Never skip or delete a test to get CI green.
- The page must work at phone width without scrolling sideways, and in light and dark mode (all colours are CSS variables on `:root`). Up to 640px wide the section tabs fill the width on one line that never wraps (tested at 320–412px with a fifth tab), each tab getting the same extra width so the gaps between labels are equal, and the light/dark button moves down, level with the first line of the title.
- **Look:** "claymorphism" chosen with the ui-ux-pro-max skill: soft 3D surfaces with 3-4px borders, double shadows (outer drop plus inner highlight), 18-28px radii, pastel tints for selected topics and lessons, and squishy buttons that scale down when pressed. Indigo `--primary` is the brand colour, `--good` green means progress and right answers. New screens reuse the tokens on `:root`: colours (`--table`, `--surface`, `--surface-2`, `--ink`, `--muted`, `--line`, `--primary`/`--primary-deep`/`--on-primary`, `--focus`, `--glow`, `--pastel-1`…`--pastel-5`, article and player colours), shadows `--clay`/`--clay-sm`/`--press`, radii `--r-s`/`--r-m`/`--r-l`, `--display` (Nunito, 800-900) for headings and German words and `--body` (DM Sans) for the rest. Controls use the `.i` line icons (`i-vol`, `i-back`, `i-book`, `i-redo`, `i-check`, `i-sun`, `i-moon`), never emoji. Tap targets are at least 44px. Section labels are sentence case, not all caps. Reuse `.sheet`, `.label`, `.seg`, `.ctl`, `.btn-main` before writing new components.

## CI and workflow

- Work on a branch and open a pull request into `main`. Don't push to `main` directly.
- Every PR runs ESLint, Playwright, `npm audit --audit-level=moderate`, `npm audit signatures` and gitleaks. CodeQL and dependency review also run, since the repo is public. Security checks also run weekly. Dependabot opens weekly updates.
- `android.yml` runs Android Lint and an emulator smoke test (`android-tests/`, Playwright driving the debug app's WebView over adb; not part of `npm test`), builds the APK on every PR (downloadable as a workflow artifact) and, on `main`, publishes it as release `app-v1.<run number>` with `sags-auf-deutsch.apk`. A run skips the release when `main` has moved on by then, since the newer run publishes its own (and tagging an older commit fails with HTTP 403 when a later merge changed a workflow). The emulator is installed in its own step with retries, because its download sometimes arrives broken. It signs with the `ANDROID_KEYSTORE_BASE64`/`ANDROID_KEYSTORE_PASSWORD` secrets, or a one-off key if they're missing (see README).
- `.gitleaks.toml` allowlists `pictures.js`, whose base64 data looks like keys.

## Where it runs

- **Web:** <https://buffalonan.github.io/german-learning/>, deployed by `pages.yml` on each merge to `main` that changes `index.html`, `pictures.js`, the illustrations or the logo (Pages source must be "GitHub Actions" in the repo settings).
- **Android app:** sideloaded from the latest GitHub release (no Play Store). It updates when a new APK is installed over it.
- **Local copy for phones:** the owner serves a copy from their computer on their home Wi-Fi (`python3 -m http.server <port> --bind 0.0.0.0`). It doesn't update by itself: copy `index.html`, `pictures.js`, `art.js`, `art/`, `logo/` and `manifest.webmanifest` over after merging, plus `node_modules/peerjs/dist/peerjs.min.js` (as `peerjs.min.js`) for Zwei Handys.
- **Claude artifact:** a hosted copy for Claude's app is the same page with the `<!doctype>`, `<html>`, `<head>` and `<body>` wrapper lines removed, plus `pictures.js`, `art.js`, the `art/` pictures and `logo/` as supporting files.
